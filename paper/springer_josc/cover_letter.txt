Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript titled "SonarMoE: Remote Prompt and Tool-Schema Reconstruction via Expert Contention in Disaggregated MoE Serving and Differentially Private Mitigation" for consideration for publication in The Journal of Supercomputing.

Modern large-scale Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) inference systems increasingly adopt Attention-FFN Disaggregation (AFD) to decouple stateful Attention Key-Value (KV) caches from shared Expert-Parallel feed-forward GPU pools. While AFD eliminates cross-tenant KV cache sharing and host co-residency, we investigate a systems-level confidentiality vulnerability arising directly from GPU SIMD/Tensor-Core matrix-multiply tile quantization and cross-rank combine-barrier synchronization.

Our main contributions are:
1. Hardware Tile-Quantization Channel Analysis (Theorem 1): We profile real FP16 expert GEMM kernels on Apple M5 Pro GPUs across OLMoE-1B-7B (+27.71 us/expert step at N=128->129) and Granite-3.1-MoE (+4.29 us/expert) and prove that sizing offline-synthesized canary micro-batches just below the tile cliff (N_can = M_tile - 6 = 122) amplifies single-probe timing capacity by 2.9x-17.8x under additive network jitter.
2. Constant-Length Multi-Model and Live SSE Evaluation (MoELeak-Bench): Under strict zero-length-leakage controls (B_vic = 64 tokens, 0.00 bits length leakage), SonarMoE achieves up to 87.0% five-domain prompt classification accuracy and 90.0% Top-1 (94.4% Top-3) accuracy across 20 Model Context Protocol (MCP) tool schemas in hardware-grounded AFD simulation, and 39.0% (1.95x random floor) over 12,928 live localhost HTTP/1.1 chunked SSE frames executing synchronized GPU expert kernels.
3. Per-Tenant Straggler-Slack Differentially Private Scheduling (OblivDispatch, Theorem 2): We design a strictly per-tenant three-stage dispatcher that hides shadow-expert overflow and shifted two-sided Gaussian count blinding (with data-independent clipping [1, N_max] and split failure accounting delta = delta_zCDP + delta_tail) inside idle cross-rank combine-barrier slack. Across all evaluated budgets and seeds, OblivDispatch achieves zero observed underflow slots, strictly obeys our analytical rho-zCDP mutual information upper bound, and dominates both Pure Router Gumbel Noise (+13.9 to +630.3 PPL degradation while still leaking 45%-68% accuracy) and Uniform Worst-Case Padding (8.0x FFN compute vs. 2.4x for OblivDispatch, +2.2% to +8.9% TPOT overhead, and only +0.08 to +0.15 PPL shift across an expanded 24-passage benchmark).

This manuscript aligns directly with The Journal of Supercomputing's focus on parallel architectures, GPU systems performance, distributed cloud AI workloads, and privacy-preserving systems engineering.
